Keith F. Prussing is a Senior Research Scientist in the Electro-Optical
Systems Laboratory at Georgia Tech Research Institute (GTRI).  He earned
his BS in 2009 and his Ph.D. in 2016 from the Georgia Institute of
Technology's School of Physics.  His current work focuses on simulating
physically realistic imagery across the electro-optical and infrared
spectrum.  He is the co-designer and co-lead developer of the Dynamic
Model Integration and Simulation Engine (DMISE) and the General
High-Fidelity Omni-Spectral Toolbox (GHOST) which aims to provide a
unified environment to simulate multi-spectral imagery of complex
environments.  Keith is also a lead developer of the Georgia Tech
Simulations Integrated Modeling System (GTSIMS) in which he works to
improve the handling of light propagation through the atmosphere,
incorporate background clutter features, and generally improve the
interoperability and efficiency of the GTSIMS framework.  Keith is also
the maintainer of Georgia Tech Signature (GTSIG) and its first principle
thermal modeling capabilities which is an integral part of signature
prediction in the infrared.  Keith is the course administrator for the
Infrared/Visible Signature Suppression course offered by EOSL as part of
the Certificate in Infrared and Electro-Optical Technology through
Georgia Tech Professional Education.  The course covers: basic signature
concerns for ground, sea, and air vehicles, modeling and analysis
techniques with a focus on the vehicle's signature, and an overview of
suppression techniques.  His research interests include near field
radiative transfer between closely spaced nanoscale objects for thermal
control, radiative transfer in complex geometries for energy balance
analysis, electromagnetic wave scattering from arbitrary geometries, and
modeling and simulation of infrared and visible light propagation for
sensor performance studies.
